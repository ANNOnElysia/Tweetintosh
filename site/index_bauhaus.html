Tweetintosh

A native Mac client for X. The same fourteen shapes become each part of it as you scroll.

macOS 15 or later. About 5 MB. Unofficial, and not made by or affiliated with X Corp. or Apple.

Part 1 of 6

A window, not a web page.

Every timeline, post and profile is drawn in SwiftUI. It follows your Mac's appearance, or you can pick one.

Part 2 of 6

Your place stays put.

New posts wait behind a pill at the top until you ask for them. Older ones load as you reach the bottom.

The app opens on an offline snapshot of your timeline, then catches up in the background. Anything that loads writes the word in a single line, so there are no spinners.

Part 3 of 6

Posts open beside the timeline.

In a wide window, a post glides into a pane next to the list, so your feed stays where you left it.

Deep press on a trackpad to peek at a profile before you open it, or to look up a word in the dictionary.

Part 4 of 6

Glass, under real light.

On macOS 26 the controls float in Liquid Glass. Earlier systems get the standard material instead.

Appearance, typeface, tint and theme colour all live in Settings.

Part 5 of 6

Five languages, woven in.

Change the language in Settings and the whole interface follows, with no restart.

These labels come from the app's own translation table. Try them, along with the serif typeface for people who would rather read in one.

    Part 6 of 6

    Build it yourself.

    You need macOS 15 or later and the Swift command line tools. Xcode is not required.

    ./build.sh
open Tweetintosh.app

    Tweetintosh signs in with your own X session and uses X's private endpoints, which can change without notice. The poster is a style study, with no connection to any Bauhaus institution.
